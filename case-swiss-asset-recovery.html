Section 1: The 2026 Offshore Wealth Landscape and Swiss Banking Realities

The Swiss private banking and international wealth management scene in 2026 is shifting from historical operational discretion to full regulatory transparency and the integration of multilateral sanctions. Statutory banking secrecy under Article 47 of the Swiss Banking Act has given way to mandatory reporting under the Automatic Exchange of Information (AEOI/CRS).

Swiss financial intermediariesâ€”including private banks, wealth managers, and life insurance firmsâ€”act as front-line enforcement authorities under oversight from the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO) and the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA). Financial institutions frequently implement precautionary ex-lege asset freezes and account restrictions using the defense of impossibility of performance under Article 119 of the Swiss Code of Obligations (CO).

Section 2: Swiss Insurance Wrappers (Unit-Linked Policies): Structure, Risks, and Claim Withholdings

Unit-linked life insurance policies (Assurance-Vie) transfer legal ownership of deposited assets (cash, equities, private equity) to a Swiss life insurance carrier under the Insurance Supervision Act (ISA/VAG) and Insurance Contract Act (ICA/VVG). While Article 17 ISA mandates a ring-fenced "tied asset" pool (gebundenes VermÃ¶gen) protecting policyholders upon carrier insolvency, policy surrender payouts are often withheld when underlying bank sub-accounts are frozen under sanctions checks, leaving investors without direct contractual recourse against custodian banks.

Section 3: Navigating Ex-Lege Asset Freezes, Sanctions, and FINMA Enforcement

In Federal Supreme Court Judgment 4A_537/2025 (April 2026), Swiss courts ruled that a "reasonable suspicion" threshold is sufficient for financial institutions to trigger administrative asset freezes under embargo regulations without requiring definitive proof of beneficial ownership. Under LP Article 44 and precedent BGE 131 III 652, administrative freeze mandates override civil debt execution, blocking civil realization until administrative unblocking or judicial relief is obtained.
