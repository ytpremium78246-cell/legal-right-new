Section 1: Introduction and San Antonio Commercial Freight Realities

San Antonio, Texas, is a critical logistics and freight transit hub in North America. It sits at the intersection of major international and interstate trade routes, leading to a high volume of heavy commercial vehicle traffic daily. Interstate 35 (I-35) acts as the main international freight highway for NAFTA and USMCA, transporting heavy goods continuously from the United States-Mexico border through Central Texas to northern industrial areas. At the same time, Interstate 10 (I-10) serves as a vital route for long-haul freight, connecting Houston, West Texas, and the Pacific Coast.

Encircling routes like Texas State Highway Loop 1604 and State Highway Loop 410 link rapidly growing suburban areas to busy commercial routes. In addition, U.S. Route 90 (US-90) supports regional freight for South Texas’s agriculture, industry, and energy sectors.

This high concentration of commercial freight traffic poses serious safety risks for occupants of passenger vehicles sharing the roads. In 2024, Bexar County reported 48,522 motor vehicle crashes, with many occurring on these main freight corridors. The reality of a collision between an 80,000-pound fully loaded truck and a 3,000-pound passenger car follows the laws of physics. The significant difference in weight and energy often leads to severe, life-changing injuries or immediate fatalities for occupants of passenger vehicles.

Section 2: Understanding Texas House Bill 19 and Divided Truck Accident Trials

The legal framework governing trucking litigation in Texas changed with House Bill 19 (HB 19), codified in Chapter 72, Subchapter B of the Texas Civil Practice and Remedies Code (CPRC §§ 72.051-72.055). This law establishes a mandatory two-part (bifurcated) trial process that corporate trucking defendants can invoke.

Trial Stage Primary Issues Decided by Jury Evidence Scope & Admissibility Strategic Exceptions
Phase 1: Compensatory Liability Driver negligence; direct crash cause; maintenance failure; compensatory damages. Limited to crash events, immediate driver actions, vehicle condition, and direct injuries. Maintenance claims proceed without driver fault. CPRC $ 72.054 statutory exceptions allow direct employer claims.
Phase 2: Exemplary Liability Direct employer negligence (hiring, training, supervision); gross negligence; punitive damages. Company safety history, USDOT/FMCSA violations, driver turnover, internal audits, corporate financials. Activated only if jury finds driver negligence or statutory Phase 1 exception.

Under CPRC $ 72.054, plaintiffs can get around Phase 1 protections and present direct employer negligence if the motor carrier entrusted the vehicle to a driver who:

  • Lacked a valid Commercial Driver's License (CDL) under 49 CFR Part 383.
  • Was disqualified from driving under 49 CFR $ 391.15 or 37 TAC $ 4.12.
  • Did not hold a valid Medical Examiner's Certificate under 49 CFR $ 391.41.
  • Had unaddressed drug/alcohol Clearinghouse violations under 49 CFR Part 382.
  • Was under an active federal or state Out-of-Service order under 49 CFR $ 390.5.
  • Was driving in violation of Hours of Service (HOS) rules under 49 CFR Part 395 at the time of the crash.

Section 3: Critical 72-Hour Evidence Preservation Strategies & FMCSR Compliance

To protect an injured client's rights, the plaintiff's legal team must implement a clear evidence preservation strategy within the first 72 hours after the crash. Standard corporate data retention schedules and telematics systems may overwrite vital ECM black box data, ELD driving logs, and video telematics in as little as 72 hours to 30 days.

Evidence Category Source System Key Forensic Data Overwrite Risk
Engine Control Module (ECM) Onboard computer (Cummins, Detroit Diesel) Pre-crash speed, throttle, brake application, RPM. Extreme: Overwritten if vehicle is moved or restarted.
Electronic Logging Device (ELD) 49 CFR Part 395 mandated telemetry GPS location, duty status changes, HOS alerts. High: Carrier databases often purge monthly.
Driver Qualification File (DQF) 49 CFR $ 391.51 fleet record PSP background reports, MVR checks, road test cards. Moderate: Missing background checks.

Section 5: Navigating Insurance Tactics, Bexar County Courts & Maximizing Recovery

Interstate commercial fleets carry layered policies from $5,000,000 to $50,000,000 alongside Self-Insured Retentions (SIRs). Trial counsel must navigate Bexar County District Courts using a structured 4-stage operational process:

  1. Stage 1: Immediate Intervention (Days 1–30): Send certified spoliation notices, seek emergency TROs in Bexar County court if needed, and conduct CVSA mechanic inspections.
  2. Stage 3: Damages Modeling & Expert Development (Months 6–12): Retain life-care planners, economists, and vocational experts under CPRC $ 41.0105 paid vs. incurred rules.
  3. Stage 4: Bifurcated Trial Execution & Resolution (Months 12–18): Execute Phase 1 compensatory trial followed by Phase 2 gross negligence exemplary damage claims.

Frequently Asked Legal Questions

What are the primary statutory deadlines for filing a claim under San Antonio 18-Wheeler Truck Litigation?
Statutes of limitations vary depending on state jurisdiction and entity status. Generally, civil injury and tort claims must be formally initiated within 2 years from the date of occurrence, whereas statutory administrative filings against governmental bodies may require notice within 90 to 180 days.
What evidence is critical for proving liability in these cases?
Crucial evidentiary elements include official regulatory inspection logs, electronic logging device (ELD) data, statutory compliance audits, eyewitness testimony, and expert accident reconstruction reports.
How does comparative negligence affect financial recovery?
Under modified comparative fault rules, an injured party may recover damages as long as their proportion of responsibility is less than 50% or 51% (depending on state statute), though total recovery is reduced proportionally by their percentage of fault.
